Permissions

Read this for: which function answers a role-table question, and when a request gets 404 or 403.

Source files: apps/diary/permissions.py, apps/diary/services/diaries.py, apps/diary/services/entries.py, apps/diary/services/invitations.py, apps/diary/services/members.py, apps/diary/services/transfers.py, apps/diary/services/deletion.py, apps/diary/views.py, apps/diary/admin.py

Every diary, membership, or entry read and write goes through this module and a service in apps/diary/services/. The checks do not read staff or superuser flags. A person who cannot read the diary or the entry gets 404, including a staff user who is not a member. A member who lacks the action gets 403. Both leave the database unchanged. The 403 page is templates/403.html.

Django admin lists every diary and membership for support. It does not call this module, and it does not list entries. Diary and membership rows stay read-only. A diary with in_custody has a custody screen. That screen is the staff exception: retain, reassign, or delete. It lists members and does not show entry text.

readable_diaries is the diaries that person belongs to. get_readable_diary raises 404 otherwise.

The role table is product_brief.md Section 4.2. These functions encode it. Entry functions take the entry's current owner. Invite, role-change, removal, leaving, entry delete, and transfer have a screen. Delete-diary does not. Entry reads and edits do.

A person sets defaults only on diaries they can read. Posting any other id returns 404 and changes nothing. Any subset may be the defaults, including none.

Invitations​

get_pending_invitation returns a pending invitation whose token matches and whose expiry is still ahead. Anything else is 404. A write locks that row.

invite_person requires a shared diary the person can read, then can_invite_or_remove_editors or can_invite_or_remove_contributors for the offered role. A private diary is 403 for its owner. A non-member and a staff user who is not a member get 404. An Editor cannot invite or replace an Editor invitation. One pending invitation per diary and email is refreshed in place. If that email already belongs to a member, nothing is sent and the form says they are already in the diary. Any other email gets the same “Invitation sent.” The message does not say whether an account exists.

The mail names the inviter, the diary, and the role, and includes the link. It does not include entry text. Accepting an existing account requires a sign-in as that email. Accepting an unknown email creates the account, its personal diary, and the membership, including while allow_registration is off. Decline and a used, revoked, or expired token are 404 on the next open. Revoke follows the same role check as invite. Each of those writes an audit row.

member_rows lists the members of a diary the person can read. pending_invitations returns only the pending rows whose role that person may revoke. A Contributor sees the member list and not the pending emails.

A shared entry shows its current owner's display name when the viewer can read that shared diary. An entry that is only in a diary they cannot read is absent, including from calendar marks and the day list.

Entries​

readable_entries is the entries in a diary that person can read. An entry in two of those diaries is one row. readable_entries_for is that queryset limited to one diary when the day page asks for one. The day page, its calendar marks, and its list of days use it. get_readable_entry raises 404 otherwise. Edit is offered on Raw view only when the viewer is the current owner, which is the same rule as can_update_entry for a row already limited to entries they can read.

can_update_entry is true only for the current owner of an entry they can read. Another member gets 403 on save. The referenced date and the diary list are part of that same save. A posted diary they cannot read returns 404. A diary they can read but cannot add to returns 403. Either way nothing is written. Diaries they cannot read stay on the entry. The posted list has to include at least one diary they can add to.

can_delete_entry is true when any one of the entry's diaries grants can_delete_own_entry or can_delete_any_entry. The current owner's delete removes the entry from every diary, including one the actor cannot read. An Owner or Editor deleting someone else's entry removes it only from the diary they name. If that was the entry's only diary, the entry is deleted. A posted diary they cannot read is 404. A diary they can read but cannot delete from is 403. Either way nothing is written. When the entry is in exactly one diary they may delete from, the post may omit the diary id. When it is in more than one, a missing diary id is 403 and changes nothing.

Roles, removal, and leaving​

change_role requires can_change_member_roles. The Owner can set another member to Editor or Contributor. The Owner's own role stays until an accepted transfer. Any other role value is 403. Posting the role the member already has changes nothing and writes no audit row. An Editor or Contributor gets 403. A non-member and a staff user who is not a member get 404.

can_remove_member is true for the Owner removing an Editor or a Contributor, and for an Editor removing a Contributor. Nobody removes the Owner, and nobody removes themself through that action. remove_member and leave_diary take that person's entries out of the diary. An entry that is already in another diary they still belong to only loses this membership. An entry that would otherwise leave them with no diary they belong to is placed in a private diary with the same name. If they already own a private diary with that name, the oldest one is used. It is not a default destination. Memberships in other diaries stay. Access is the membership row, checked on the request, so the next request from an open session is 404. The Owner cannot leave. That is 403 and changes nothing.

Each of those writes an audit row: role_changed, member_removed, member_left, entry_removed, or entry_deleted. A role change does not record the previous role. When the diary being left is shared, each entry that leaves it also writes entry_moved. None of the rows store diary text.

Transfers​

can_transfer_ownership is the Owner. request_ownership stores one pending transfer for the diary, addressed to an email, and sends that email a link. Choosing a member uses that member's email. The confirmation is “Transfer requested.” for every address, including one with no account. The page does not say whether an account exists. A second pending transfer is refused until the first is accepted, declined, or cancelled. Sending the transfer to your own email is refused and stores nothing. A private diary can be transferred. The shared mark does not change.

accept_ownership requires a sign-in as that email. It does not create an account. The wrong account gets 404 and is not shown the diary name. In one transaction the current Owner becomes an Editor, then the recipient's membership becomes Owner, or a new Owner membership is created. The partial unique constraint is never asked to hold two Owner rows. If the new row fails, the demotion rolls back. Decline is the token holder, unless they are signed in as a different account, which is 404. Cancel is the Owner. After acceptance the previous Owner can leave.

request_entry_transfer is the current owner. One pending transfer per entry. The same confirmation rule applies, and the email does not include the entry text. Accept changes owner to the recipient and leaves original_submitter and the raw text as they are. The entry stays in diaries the recipient already belongs to. It is removed from the others. If that leaves none, it is placed in the recipient's default diary, or their oldest private diary. A shared diary added or removed by that handoff writes entry_moved before the owner changes. The signed-in recipient can read the text on the accept page. A signed-out visit and the email do not include it. The wrong account gets 404. Cancel is the current owner.

Saving an entry with a different diary list is the move. It does not change the current owner. When a shared diary is added or removed, that writes entry_moved and does not store the text. A move between private diaries writes no row. A leaving member proposes an entry transfer from the entry after leaving. There is no separate list of entries on the leave form.

How the save and the revision check behave is on Entries and autosave. can_edit_attributes has no screen. Organization attributes are not built. can_delete_diary has no member screen. Staff delete a custody diary from the admin screen, not through that check.

Account deletion and custody​

diaries_needing_successor is the shared diaries this person owns that still have another member. Closing an account is deactivate_account. It refuses while that list is non-empty unless the person chooses custody. Custody cancels a pending ownership transfer, sets in_custody, and writes custody_opened. The person remains Owner until the grace period ends or staff reassign. Sign-in, the password form, and the grace period are on Authentication.

retain_custody, reassign_custody, and delete_custody_diary require a staff actor and a diary that is in custody. Anyone else is refused, and a diary that is not in custody is 404. Reassign demotes the current Owner to Editor, then makes the chosen active member Owner, and clears the flag. Delete removes the diary and entries that belonged only to it. An entry that also sits in another diary loses this membership and keeps the rest. Each action writes an audit row. Deleting the diary clears the diary link on that row and leaves the row.

reinstate_account is staff-only. It clears custody on diaries the person still owns. erase_expired_accounts removes the account after the grace period and leaves a shared diary in custody with no Owner row. A second call changes nothing more.
