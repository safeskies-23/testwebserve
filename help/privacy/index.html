Privacy

Pebble Diary is a private diary. This page says who can read an entry, what the service itself can read, and how long a deleted account can be restored. The same page is linked from the footer and from Settings.

Who can read an entry​

You can read the entries in your diaries. A private diary is only yours. A shared diary is also readable by the people invited to it, and an entry there shows the writer's name. The app does not show diary text to site staff, and a staff sign-in does not open someone else's diary. A person who is not a member of a diary gets the same “page not found” as a missing page. They do not learn that the diary exists.

An entry that is only in your private diary stays there. Putting the same entry in a shared diary lets the members of that diary read it. It is still one entry.

The service can read what you write​

This is not end-to-end encryption. The service reads an entry in order to save it and show it to you, including on another device. When you open the site over HTTPS, that connection is encrypted. The database is encrypted where it is stored. Encryption in transit and at rest does not stop the service, or someone who hosts the database and holds the keys, from reading entries.

What the app does not do with the text​

Diary text is not sent to an external AI service. Diary text is not added to the logs used to run the site.

On a phone or computer that is yours, the browser keeps a copy of an unsaved entry until the save is confirmed, you sign out, or 14 days pass. Signing in with “This is a shared device” does not keep that copy. Signing out removes copies this browser kept.

Backups, retention, and deletion​

You can delete your account from Settings. Enter your password. If you own a shared diary that still has other people in it, transfer ownership from Members and wait until they accept. If no one can take it, choose site-administrator custody. That diary is flagged for the person who runs the site. They can keep it, give it to a member, or delete it. That screen does not show entries.

Deleting the account signs you out. You cannot sign in. Your private diaries are hidden. An entry you wrote in a shared diary stays there under your name. That name is no longer a way to sign in.

For 90 days, a site administrator can restore the account. Private diaries come back. Memberships you still had come back. A shared diary in site-administrator custody comes back to you. A diary someone already accepted stays with them.

After 90 days the account is erased. Private diaries, and entries that were only in them, are erased. A shared entry stays, with your name and the words you wrote. The erased account leaves no email, account number, or link. A deleted account cannot be reconnected.

The site does not keep database backups today. When it does, a backup can still hold an erased entry for up to 90 days after the live copy is gone. Then that backup copy expires.

Risks that remain​

  • The service can read what you write. This is not end-to-end encryption.
  • Someone with your unlocked, signed-in phone or computer can read your diaries. Sign out on a shared computer. Changing your password signs out other sessions.
  • An operator, or the company that hosts the database, can read content if they have the data or the keys. Access to that is limited. It cannot be ruled out.
  • There is no second sign-in factor. Your password, and the email account that can reset it, are what keep other people out. A reset link works once and expires after one hour.
